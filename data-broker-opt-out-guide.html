How to Find and Delete Your Profile From Commercial Data Brokers

Most people assume that if they never type their name into a website, their browsing stays anonymous. In reality, commercial data brokers connect anonymous phone and browser signals back to real household names using three everyday bridges—and then sell those combined profiles to marketers, background-check firms, and government contractors.

1. How Anonymous Browser Clues Connect to Your Real Name

  • Step 1 — Your Phone's Advertising ID: Every iPhone and Android device comes with a built-in advertising serial number. Everyday weather, flashlight, and game apps share that ID alongside your GPS location whenever they load an ad.
  • Step 2 — Your Home Wi-Fi Address: When your phone and your laptop connect to the same home Wi-Fi network, tracking networks observe both devices sharing the exact same internet address every evening.
  • Step 3 — Public Records & Shopping Lists: Data brokers merge your household internet address and phone advertising ID with voter rolls, property deeds, warranty cards, and retail loyalty lists to attach a full name and street address to your device clues.

2. What Federal Regulators Have Found

Over the past several years, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) and federal inspectors general have issued landmark enforcement orders and watchdog audits exposing how commercial location brokers operate:

  • FTC v. Outlogic / X-Mode (Docket No. C-4802): First federal order banning a data broker from selling sensitive location trails collected through software kits embedded inside ordinary mobile apps.
  • FTC v. Mobilewalla (Docket No. C-4813): Prohibited a broker from retaining consumer location histories scooped out of split-second ad auctions without user consent.
  • DHS Watchdog Audit (Report OIG-23-61): Confirmed that federal agencies purchased commercial smartphone location feeds without obtaining a judicial warrant first.

3. Three Free Steps to Cut Off the Data Pipeline

Step 1: Turn Off Your Phone's Advertising ID (30 Seconds)

On iPhone: Open Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking and turn off Allow Apps to Request to Track. On Android: Open Settings → Security & Privacy → Privacy → Ads and tap Delete advertising ID.

Step 2: Use Free Centralized Deletion Portals

Under California's Delete Act, the California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) operates the free DROP portal at privacy.ca.gov, allowing residents to submit one centralized deletion request across hundreds of registered commercial data brokers. Residents in other states can send formal deletion demands under state privacy laws (such as Virginia, Colorado, Texas, and Oregon) or opt out directly through major broker portals like LexisNexis, Acxiom, and Oracle.
