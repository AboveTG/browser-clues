Why Clearing Cookies No Longer Stops Device Tracking

For years, the standard privacy advice was simple: open Incognito mode or clear your browser cookies. While clearing cookies erases small ID files saved on your hard drive, modern tracking scripts no longer need to store anything on your computer to recognize you when you come back.

1. What a "Device Clue" Actually Is

To display webpages properly, your browser automatically shares basic technical facts with every website you visit. For example, a site needs to know how wide your screen is so buttons fit properly, and what time zone you are in so local news or event times display accurately.

Individually, none of those details identify you—millions of people live in the Eastern Time Zone or use a 1080p laptop screen. When combined, however, six or seven ordinary settings narrow down your exact device like a digital signature:

  • Graphics Card Signature: Your browser reports the exact model and driver version of your graphics chip, which varies across phone generations and computer models.
  • Processor Cores & Memory Tier: Websites can read how many processor cores your device has and roughly how much memory is available.
  • Screen Dimensions & Pixel Density: Even resizing a browser window leaves behind your monitor's full screen resolution and sharpness multiplier.
  • Installed Fonts & Language Settings: Your exact list of system fonts and preferred language codes add extra uniqueness.

2. Why Incognito Mode Doesn't Hide Hardware Clues

Private or Incognito windows stop your browser from saving your browsing history and cookies after you close the tab. However, while the window is open, your graphics chip, screen sharpness, processor cores, and home internet address remain identical to your normal browser window.

3. Browsers That Actively Mask Device Clues

If you want to blend into the crowd instead of standing out with a unique hardware signature, choose a browser that standardizes what websites see:

  • Brave Browser: Automatically adds subtle randomization to graphics and audio clues on every site so trackers cannot link visits together.
  • Firefox (with Strict Tracking Protection): Blocks known fingerprinting scripts and masks common hardware identifiers.
  • Safari (iPhone & Mac): Presents a simplified, uniform hardware profile to websites so every iPhone of the same model looks nearly identical.
